# {% include icon.html icon="fa-solid fa-circle-info" %}About SEARCH Lab
{: .page-title }

**SEARCH Lab** (Space Exploration Architecture Laboratory) studies space mission architecture design and the demonstration of key technologies across missions ranging from low Earth orbit to lunar, planetary, and deep-space exploration. We translate scientific and engineering exploration objectives into orbits and trajectories, subsystem requirements, and integrated operational scenarios, and verify the feasibility of optimized design alternatives through multidisciplinary analysis.
{: .page-intro }

Mission architecture is an integrated systems design that connects **orbits and trajectories** with propulsion, structures, thermal control, power, communications, payloads, and ground operations to form a cohesive mission. We comprehensively consider the performance and constraints of each subsystem to assess satellite configurations and maneuvers, observation and communications schedules, orbit insertion, and landing conditions in detail.

To support this work, SEARCH Lab builds on **astrodynamics and high-fidelity orbit analysis, dynamical systems and multi-body astrodynamics, numerical optimization and optimal control, and mission architecture and systems analysis** as a shared theoretical foundation. We apply these core methods across six fields: **Earth-orbit mission design; lunar and planetary exploration; atmospheric entry, landing, and thermal protection; solar sails and deep-space exploration; deep-space optical communications; and CubeSat in-space demonstration**. See [Research]({{ '/en/research/' | relative_url }}) for details of each area.

Student researchers conduct research linked to national R&D programs and deep-space exploration projects at the Korea Aerospace Research Institute (KARI). Their research topics and project schedules determine the scope of their involvement and responsibilities. They learn engineering practices through assigned work in mission design, systems analysis, performance verification, and the development of flight operations concepts. In CubeSat demonstration projects, they gain experience in verifying designs through hardware fabrication, ground environmental testing, and in-orbit operations according to the development stage and their assigned responsibilities.

Students take the initiative in using modern analysis and simulation tools, including Python, MATLAB, STK, and GMAT, to address their research questions. They assess the rigor of physical models and numerical results and develop practical engineering skills in deriving system requirements from analytical findings. [For Students]({{ '/en/students/' | relative_url }}) describes the research and supervision process in more detail.

We welcome students who want to design missions from Earth orbit to deep space and develop research ideas into technology demonstrations.
