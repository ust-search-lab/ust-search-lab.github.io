# {% include icon.html icon="fa-solid fa-circle-info" %}About SEARCH Lab
{: .page-title }

SEARCH Lab (Space Exploration ARCHitecture Laboratory) studies space mission architectures and the demonstration of key technologies, from Earth orbit to lunar, planetary, and deep-space exploration. We design orbits and trajectories, spacecraft system configurations, and operational scenarios to meet mission objectives, and evaluate the performance and feasibility of design alternatives through analysis and simulation.

## Research direction

Our work draws on astrodynamics and dynamical systems theory, together with guidance and attitude control, numerical optimization, AI and machine learning, and systems analysis. We study designs that meet mission requirements by considering how orbital and maneuver plans affect propellant use, power, communications, observations, and operations.

Our main research areas are Earth-orbit mission design; lunar and planetary mission design; atmospheric entry, landing, and thermal protection; solar sails and deep-space exploration; deep-space optical communications; and CubeSat development and in-space demonstration. See [Research]({{ '/en/research/' | relative_url }}) for the theoretical foundations and details of each area.

</div>

<div class="about-section" markdown="1">

## Collaboration and verification

SEARCH Lab draws on the expertise of its principal investigator and collaborating researchers to conduct research linked to national R&D programs and space exploration projects at the Korea Aerospace Research Institute (KARI). Depending on the research topic, we bring together experience in theoretical research, numerical analysis, and system development to address design and verification challenges in actual missions.

We assess assumptions in physical models and the validity of numerical results, and incorporate analytical findings into mission requirements, system designs, and operational plans. We also study how CubeSat ground testing and in-space demonstrations can verify the applicability of design methods and key technologies.

</div>

<div class="about-section" markdown="1">

## Student research participation

Student researchers participate in the lab's research projects, contributing to mission design, systems analysis, performance verification, and the development of operational concepts according to their research topics and the stage of each project. CubeSat demonstration projects can provide opportunities to verify designs through hardware fabrication, ground environmental testing, and in-orbit operations. Through this work, students develop engineering skills that connect analysis and simulation results with actual design, testing, and operations.

</div>
