About SEARCH Lab

SEARCH Lab (Space Exploration ARCHitecture Laboratory) studies space mission architecture design and the demonstration of key technologies across missions ranging from low Earth orbit to lunar, planetary, and deep-space exploration. We translate scientific and engineering exploration objectives into orbits and trajectories, subsystem requirements, and integrated operational scenarios, and evaluate the performance and feasibility of design alternatives through multidisciplinary analysis.

Mission architecture is an integrated systems design that connects orbits and trajectories with attitude, propulsion, structures, thermal control, power, communications, payloads, and ground operations. We consider subsystem performance and constraints together to assess satellite configurations and maneuvers, observation and communications schedules, orbit insertion, and landing conditions.

SEARCH Lab’s shared core theories and methods are Astrodynamics and High-Fidelity Orbit Analysis; Dynamical Systems and Multi-Body Astrodynamics; Guidance, Control, and Numerical Optimization; Attitude Dynamics and Control; AI and Machine Learning Applications; Mission Architecture and Systems Analysis. We apply them across six fields: Earth-Orbit Mission Design; Lunar and Planetary Mission Design; Atmospheric Entry, Landing, and Thermal Protection; Solar Sails and Deep-Space Exploration; Deep-Space Optical Communications; CubeSat Development and In-Space Demonstration. See Research for the theoretical foundations and details of each area.

Student researchers conduct research linked to national R&D programs and deep-space exploration projects at the Korea Aerospace Research Institute (KARI). Their research topics and project schedules determine the scope of their involvement and responsibilities. They learn engineering practices through assigned work in mission design, systems analysis, performance verification, and the development of flight operations concepts. In CubeSat demonstration projects, they gain experience in verifying designs through hardware fabrication, ground environmental testing, and in-orbit operations according to the development stage and their assigned responsibilities.

Students use analysis and simulation tools, including Python, MATLAB, STK, and GMAT, to address their research questions. They assess model assumptions and the validity of numerical results, developing engineering skills in translating analytical findings into system requirements. For Students describes the learning and research process in more detail.

We welcome students who want to design missions from Earth orbit to deep space and develop research ideas into technology demonstrations.
